HotRank UI/UX — Granular Visual Description
The current interface is a dark, cinematic ranking application rather than a traditional marketing website. It feels closer to a premium editorial dashboard, creative culture magazine, and leaderboard combined.
The primary visual system is defined in [`src/index.css`](/Users/Ira/Desktop/HOTRANK/src/index.css):
Near-black background: #050505
Charcoal panels and cards
White typography for hierarchy
Muted zinc-gray supporting text
Purple-to-pink gradients for brand energy
Emerald for rising/positive signals
Yellow for pending/editorial review
Amber for monetizable prompt content
Playfair Display italic serif for editorial headlines
Inter for interface text
JetBrains Mono for technical/ranking metadata
The whole application is viewport-contained: the browser window uses a full-height layout with internal scrolling instead of a conventional long page. This is controlled in [`src/App.tsx`](/Users/Ira/Desktop/HOTRANK/src/App.tsx) with h-screen, overflow-hidden, and scrollable page panels.
Global shell
Every page sits inside the same top-level shell.
Header
The navigation bar is black, horizontally spaced, and separated from the content by a thin zinc border.
On desktop it contains:
Left: italic, bold HOTRANK AI wordmark
Center-left: uppercase navigation links:Rankings
Submit
Creators
Insights

Right:Live Global Heat pill showing 98.4k
Search icon
Sign In button, or signed-in profile identity
Logout icon when authenticated

The brand mark is white for HOTRANK, while AI receives a purple-to-pink gradient treatment.
The navigation is intentionally compact and editorial. It does not look like a consumer social app with large icon buttons; it looks more like a private cultural index or premium creative intelligence tool.
Mobile header
On smaller screens:
The desktop navigation links disappear
A search icon remains
The sign-in action becomes part of the menu
A hamburger icon opens a full-width dark dropdown panel beneath the header
The menu has generous vertical spacing and uppercase tracking
Signed-in users see their avatar/email and a Sign Out action inside the expanded menu
The mobile menu is not a side drawer. It appears as a dark, full-width overlay directly below the header.
1. Homepage / Rankings page
Route: /
Source: [`src/pages/Home.tsx`](/Users/Ira/Desktop/HOTRANK/src/pages/Home.tsx)
The homepage is the strongest and most complete expression of the HotRank concept.
It is not a typical hero-then-content landing page. It is structured as a two-column live ranking workspace.
Homepage hero / upper view
Overall composition
On desktop, the content is centered in a max-width container and split approximately:
Left: 55%
Right: 45%
The top of the content begins about 32 pixels below the header.
There is no large marketing headline such as “Discover the future of AI video.” Instead, the first thing the visitor encounters is the ranked work itself.
This makes the product feel active and editorial immediately.
Left-side hero: rank-one spotlight
The left column is the visual hero.
It presents the highest-ranked approved video using [`SpotlightRankOne.tsx`](/Users/Ira/Desktop/HOTRANK/src/components/ranking/SpotlightRankOne.tsx).
The visual treatment is designed to make rank one feel materially more important than every other submission:
Large media area
Prominent preview image or playable embed
Strong contrast against the black background
Oversized rank indicator
Heat Score emphasis
Creator attribution
Engagement and trend context
Save and interaction controls
Editorial or ranking labels
The spotlight is designed to answer immediately:
What is currently number one?
Who made it?
Why is it hot?
What is its score?
Is it rising or falling?
Can I save or open the work?
The card feels like a featured magazine cover mixed with a sports leaderboard’s number-one position.
Visual hierarchy inside the spotlight
The visual order is approximately:
Rank-one identity
Media preview
Work title
Creator
Heat score
Supporting score metrics
Why-it-ranks explanation
Interaction controls
The title uses the serif italic face, helping distinguish editorial content from interface metadata.
The score system uses large, high-contrast numeric typography. Supporting data is deliberately smaller and uppercase, keeping the main emotional focus on the work itself rather than turning the page into a spreadsheet.
Empty hero state
If there are no approved rankings, the left side becomes a large rounded container with:
Subtle zinc border
Transparent charcoal background
Centered italic serif text
Message: No approved rankings yet
This state is quiet rather than alarming. It preserves the premium tone instead of displaying an error-heavy admin message.
Homepage middle view

The Rankings

The heading uses white italic serif typography, creating an editorial contrast with the dense list below.
At the top-right of the ranking column is the count of approved entries, displayed as small uppercase metadata.

The section is visually marked by:
Yellow uppercase label: Pending Review
Small animated yellow dot

The rows use dark zinc surfaces rather than bright cards. Hovering creates a subtle contrast shift and makes the selected item feel interactive.

Each row communicates:

Interaction affordances
The rank number is intentionally oversized and muted at rest, becoming brighter on hover. This creates a strong visual rhythm down the list.
The score is placed toward the right side and uses high-contrast white or status color. Supporting labels are tiny, uppercase, and widely tracked.
This is a leaderboard first and a card grid second.
Selected-content interaction
Clicking a spotlight or ranking row opens the DetailReveal overlay.
The interaction is modal rather than navigational, allowing the visitor to inspect a clip without losing their position in the ranking feed.
The selected item is also synchronized with the URL query parameter when an unlock flow returns to the homepage.
Homepage lower view
The homepage does not have a conventional full-width lower section. Instead, the right ranking column continues into two compact lower elements.
Ranking Source panel
A small dark panel appears under the ranking list on desktop.
It contains:

Approved count
Pending count
This panel feels like an audit/readout strip. It reinforces that the rankings are data-backed rather than manually presented.
Footer/readout strip
The homepage footer is very small and technical.
It includes:

Note that approved ranks use deterministic Heat Score V2
Animated pink status dot
Live from Supabase
This is not a brand footer. It is a live system-status footer.
The effect is closer to a broadcast control room or editorial intelligence dashboard than to a normal website footer.
Mobile homepage behavior
On mobile:
The two-column layout collapses vertically
The rank-one spotlight appears first
The ranking list appears underneath
The ranking section occupies a shorter viewport-height panel
Horizontal density is reduced
Some metadata is hidden
Scrolling remains internal to the content areas
The mobile experience preserves the hierarchy:
Number one
Rankings
Supporting system status

Overall UX character
The interface communicates:
Editorial authority
Live ranking pressure
AI-native creative culture
Premium creator identity
Data-backed heat
Controlled exclusivity
Future commerce
Its strongest UX pattern is the repeated two-column structure:
Left: featured, editorial, or explanatory content
Right: ranked, analytical, or market-facing content
Its strongest interaction pattern is progressive reveal:
Feed row
Detail overlay
Prompt/recipe depth
Creator identity
Unlock or interaction layer
Its main UX weakness is density. The app contains substantial information—scores, signals, creator data, prompt metadata, workflow notes, statuses, and commerce states—inside a relatively compact viewport. This creates a sophisticated feel, but new users may need stronger onboarding and clearer explanation of what “Heat,” “Ignite,” “Official,” “Approved,” and “Prompt Capsule” mean.
